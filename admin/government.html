§ I — The premise

How Lemon Business strengthens the culture of payment in Singapore.

A counterparty-reputation platform aimed at the B2B trade that the credit bureaus only ever see as a default statistic. The mechanism is private-sector infrastructure — but the outcomes are public-good: prompter payment, fewer phoenix companies, fewer SMEs sunk by a single bad debt, and a market where good-faith operators are visibly distinguishable from bad-faith ones.

“Lemon Business is private-sector infrastructure for B2B counterparty trust — turning observed payment conduct, not just bank-derived default scores, into a signal SMEs can act on, with an evidence requirement, a contest window and a right of reply built into the architecture.”

The one-line frame. Lead every policy conversation with it.

§ II — The market mechanism

Three forces, all policy-aligned.

Prompt payment becomes economically rational. Good-faith dealing becomes worth doing. Transparency lowers the cost of trade credit. All three are wired into the Lemon Score and reduce it; all three map to existing Singapore-government priorities for a healthy SME economy.

Force 01
Prompt payment becomes economically rational.

Late payment, bad-faith invoice disputes and deposit-and-disappear stop being free externalities. The corroboration multiplier — quadratic — ensures one supplier is a complaint, two is a pattern. Owners internalise that their payment conduct follows their UEN, not just the entity they can dissolve. Phoenixing — the same director sinking entity after entity, leaving unpaid sub-contractors behind — becomes self-correcting at the market level.

Force 02
Good-faith dealing becomes worth doing.

Settling an overdue invoice in full, holding a bizSAFE or ISO accreditation, and a clean trade history are first-class Lemon-Score reducers — verifiable and third-party-confirmed. For the first time, an SME's reputation for fair dealing — not just its bank's view of its solvency — becomes a visible, improvable asset that travels with its UEN.

Force 03
Transparency lowers the cost of trade credit.

When counterparty conduct is observable, suppliers extend terms with less risk and SMEs with clean records win better terms. That directly attacks the bad-debt write-offs — rising from ~4% to ~6% of B2B invoices (Atradius, 2024→2025) — that sink cash-strapped SMEs. Lower information asymmetry, by mechanism rather than exhortation.

§ IV — Pre-empting the questions

Four questions every agency will ask. Answers ready.

“Is this a blacklist?”

No. Bidirectional, decayed, evidence-required, contestable, appealable, time-limited. Functionally closer to a commercial credit bureau than to a blacklist; the Singapore Commercial Credit Bureau, Credit Bureau Asia and Experian-DP are the live within-SG precedents — already operating, already governed.

“Defamation / POHA exposure?”

Built to a facts-only standard: evidence at intake, reasonable care, every entry dated and keyed to a UEN, real chance to respond. The contest window is a built-in right of reply. Singapore has no anti-SLAPP shield, so the architecture leans on verifiable fact — "invoice #X was 94 days overdue", never "he's a cheat".

“Is it anti-competitive?”

CCCS-aware by design. An information platform that increases transparency is not a coordinated refusal to deal. Each business decides independently. The contest window and right of reply distinguish it from a concerted boycott or a private cartel ledger.

“Doesn't this hurt small operators most?”

The architecture protects them. A clean record now travels with the SME — a peach is identified, not just a lemon. The party owed money is usually the smaller one, and it finally has a record. The routes back (settling the debt, accreditation, time decay) are low-friction. Checking your own score is free. Appeals are free for both sides. No business pays to clear a fair record.

§ V — The asks

Five steps. Sequenced. No agency asked twice.

A consultative pre-engagement before MVP, then sandboxed validation, then code-of-practice formalisation. Mirrors how a commercial credit bureau matured: from industry pilot → guidance → de-facto reference.

  1. Informal pre-consultation with PDPC, ACRA and Enterprise Singapore before the MVP build. Validate the evaluative-purpose handling and the UEN-binding architecture; capture early concerns; revise before code rather than after.
  2. CCCS guidance that an evidence-tied, contestable information platform is pro-competitive, not a concerted practice. A short comfort note de-risks the whole category.
  3. SBF / trade-association briefing of the counterparty-side features — claimed profile, counter-review, contest window, free appeal, decay path. Sign-off on the bidirectional design before any sector pilot.
  4. Sandbox pilot in one contained sector — construction sub-contracting (SOPA adjudication seed data) or wholesale distribution recommended. Quarterly review against four metrics: average days overdue, dispute-resolution rate, bad-debt write-off rate, settlements logged.
  5. Industry code of practice with Lemon Business as default infrastructure for B2B counterparty-conduct data — analogous to how the SCCB became the de-facto reference for commercial credit. End-state: a trade norm, not just a startup.
§ VI — Why 2026, specifically

Four converging windows.

Bad debt rising. Atradius: B2B bad-debt write-offs climbed from ~4% to ~6% of invoices (2024→2025) even as late payment improved. The permanent-loss number — the one that actually sinks SMEs — is worsening, and nothing today prices it in.
Insolvencies surging. MinLaw recorded 307 compulsory windings-up in 2024, up ~52.7% year-on-year. Counterparty risk is front-of-mind for every finance team extending terms — exactly the moment a conduct signal lands.
ACRA registry modernised. The BizFile relaunch (Dec 2024) makes UEN and officer data more programmatically accessible. The identity substrate Lemon Business binds to is ready and public.
Prompt payment on the agenda. SOPA, the SBF Prompt Payment code and Enterprise Singapore's cashflow focus keep B2B payment behaviour in policy conversation. The window for a private-sector conduct layer is open.
§ VII — Artefacts to circulate

What to hand over, in which order.

Once policy outreach begins, three artefacts get circulated in sequence. None of them require the platform to exist yet — they describe the architecture, the legal posture, and the policy alignment.

  • This brief — the institutional-register write-up. Circulate first.
  • The legal memo (confidential, by counsel) — defamation & POHA false-statement analysis, PDPA evaluative-purpose mapping, CCCS pro-competitive checklist. Circulate to legal contacts only.
  • The full Intel Cabinet PDF (compiled from the other admin pages — competitors, market sizing, pricing, whitespace, design audit). Circulate to senior policy contacts who want the full picture.
§ VIII — Closing principle
“The job is not to punish struggling businesses. The job is to make prompt payment and good-faith dealing economically rational for the operator who has, until now, paid no price for stiffing a supplier. That is the cultural shift Lemon Business is built to produce.”

Use this when the conversation drifts toward enforcement. Lemon Business is incentive design, not enforcement.
